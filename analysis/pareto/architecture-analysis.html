What we learned

Four MACs win on speed and energy.

They deliver 2.12× throughput and 42.8% lower energy for 19.9% more cell area than one MAC.

The smallest design is not the lowest-energy design.

Fixed clock and controller activity is paid for 159 cycles with one MAC and only 75 cycles with four.

Utilization falls as lane count rises.

Derived MAC utilization drops from 75.7% to 43.8% because the serial attention phases occupy a larger fraction of the shorter schedule.

Four MACs are enough for this problem shape.

Four tokens expose four independent QKT scores. More lanes require parallel query rows or a larger workload.

Evidence boundary

Measured

Cycles, outputs, activity, tool-reported post-route power estimate, cell area, timing, DRC, and LVS for the three implemented variants.

Derived

Speedup, energy reduction, utilization, the 47-cycle serial floor, and the Amdahl scaling curve.

Hypothesis

A score-parallel 2-MAC schedule could reach about 103 cycles and 97,087 attention/s. Power and area are not predicted.

Next controlled experiment

Keep the workload, arithmetic, memory, MAC count, and clock fixed. Change only the 2-MAC QKT scheduler so two key scores run together. This tests whether scheduling, rather than additional arithmetic, can recover the 16-cycle gap.

Power is an activity-aware post-route OpenSTA estimate from the measured gate-level VCD, not a silicon measurement. Maximum-slew warnings remain; the 2- and 4-MAC layouts also retain maximum-capacitance warnings. Official TinyTapeout submission checks remain required.
